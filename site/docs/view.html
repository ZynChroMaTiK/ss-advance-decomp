H-toggle third-person camera

src/view.c adds the requested PC camera feature. It is new native code, not a recovered GBA routine or an exact reproduction of a particular Serious Sam PC release's camera tuning. Existing matching C, movement, jump and original camera-height routines are unchanged.

Initialize SsaView once per local player with ssa_view_init. First person is the default. Feed normalized H key events to ssa_view_key; H and h are accepted. Each fresh enabled press switches first/third person. Holding the key or OS autorepeat does not toggle again. Key release rearms the action. Disabled input, including menus/text entry, consumes a press without changing mode; enabling controls while it remains held does not trigger it. Call ssa_view_focus_lost on focus loss to clear the held latch, preserving mode.

The playable Windows frontend supplies keydown/up and repeat status through its PC input adapter. H toggles the actual camera and rendered Sam body.

Camera behavior

After horizontal movement, vertical/jump simulation and ssa_camera_step, call ssa_view_pose_pitch with the resulting player body, original eye state, heading and aim pitch. The zero-pitch ssa_view_pose wrapper remains available. The heading convention matches the recovered game: zero faces +Y, 0x4000 faces +X. First person returns the exact player X/Y and recovered eye Z.

Third person requests a position 256 world units behind the player and 64 above the eye. Its look target is 256 units ahead of the player's eye. distance and lift are tunable signed 16.16 values, bounded to 0..256 and 0..64 units. The rear boom now orbits with mouse pitch; the lift stays in world-up. This preserves the default level framing while keeping the camera behind the player at steep downward angles. Native PC presentation traces the real weapon contact and turns the displaced camera toward it, aligning the original centered crosshair with the shot. Player weapon aim is unchanged by this convergence. The original reference renderer remains horizontal. See chase aiming.

A four-unit camera clearance volume moves from the eye toward the desired position in swept steps of at most one unit per axis. It checks static walls, portal openings, floors and ceilings using the recovered map query. The boom stops before the first obstruction. If less than 32 units fit, the actual pose falls back to first person while preserving the user's third-person preference. It expands again when space clears. Missing/invalid collision data also falls back to the eye view. The sweep is deliberately conservative and does not resolve dynamic enemies, moving entities or near-plane/frustum clipping.

Give the camera a dedicated SsaMapCollision workspace. Its queries overwrite that workspace's temporary contacts and heights. Do not dispatch those contacts as gameplay events, and do not overwrite a pending movement query with camera queries. The camera never moves the player, changes heading or modifies the original eye state. Jumping changes eye Z before pose construction, so both view modes follow takeoff and landing.

Rendering integration

SsaViewPose contains position, look target, actual third-person flag, effective boom distance and obstruction flag. The renderer should show the player body only for actual third person, hide the first-person weapon in that mode, and restore the first-person weapon on eye-view fallback. Gameplay aim remains anchored to the player. The base pose supplies a provisional point along aim; SsaNativeGame.pc_pose replaces it with the dedicated exact-contact query for PC camera convergence. Its pc_camera/render_pitch are independent from the original reference camera and the player's weapon orientation.

The playable frontend renders Sam's original body/animation and hides the first-person weapon during actual chase view, restoring it on eye-view fallback. Both the original reference renderer and dynamic PC renderer consume this pose.

Validation

Run powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\decomp.ps1 test-view. Native tests compile with warnings-as-errors, AddressSanitizer and undefined behavior checks, using a non-PIE executable to avoid local WSL sanitizer startup stalls. They cover H press/release/repeat, disabled controls, focus loss, default mode, four cardinal headings, body/eye immutability, wall retraction, close-wall fallback, portal floors/ceilings, automatic expansion, missing map handling, configuration bounds and coordinate saturation.

The local WSL test repeatedly stalled with default leak scanning, while the same executable and driver completed with ASAN_OPTIONS=detect_leaks=0. The test runner therefore defaults to disabling leak scanning, retaining AddressSanitizer and undefined-behavior checks. Explicit caller ASAN options can override this. The child test has a 60-second timeout. Leak freedom is not claimed by this validation; the selected sanitizer options are in the report.

They also simulate complete jumps and H toggles at all sixteen real player starts: 2,048 camera frames, including destination clearance checks. Total: 10,962 assertions, including128 additional pitched clearance poses across the same16 maps. Results and source hashes are saved in analysis/native-view-validation.json. These are new-feature behavior tests, not byte matches or differential comparisons with original third-person code.

The updated 256-unit rear offset,64-unit lift and pitch orbit pass these checks. The rebuilt Windows frontend also passed its 600-frame gameplay smoke test and the unchanged 5,258-frame level-completion check. The current chase snapshot is build/native-game-smoke/gameplay.bmp; results are in analysis/windows-native-game-validation.json.
