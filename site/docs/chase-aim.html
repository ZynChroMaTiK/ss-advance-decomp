Third-person aim alignment

The PC chase camera now moves with aim pitch and points toward the common sight ray's contact point. The original centered crosshair artwork, target animation and visibility rules remain unchanged.

ssa_view_pose_pitch rotates the rear boom while keeping the configured lift in world-up: the horizontal rear distance is distance*cos(pitch) and vertical offset is lift-distance*sin(pitch). At level aim this retains the256-unit rear distance and64-unit height bias. It never swings in front of the player at steep downward angles. The existing four-unit clearance sweep, one-unit substeps, retraction and close-wall first-person fallback remain active. Jumping moves the pivot with the live eye. The original ssa_view_pose API retains zero-pitch behavior for existing callers.

SsaNativeGame.camera_trace owns separate query storage. During view preparation it traces the player's real weapon heading, shoot height and slope along a common unlimited sight ray for every weapon. Free aim uses full yaw precision and live eye height; the original aiming policy uses its body-facing heading,96-unit shoot height and current auto-aim slope. The new ssa_weapon_hit_query_unlimited with exact=1 returns contact coordinates without the original wall/entity offset used for impact particles. It applies no damage or script effects and cannot overwrite the firing trace. No-hit rays use their map-boundary endpoint. Camera convergence is independent of attack reach: switching to the chainsaw no longer pulls the target closer and tilts the camera downward. Weapon 1 retains96-unit melee reach; ranged attacks now have no distance cap. The ROM range table is preserved for the original routines and nearby muzzle placement. See unlimited range.

ssa_view_angles turns the displaced camera toward that point using ROM angle tables without discarding coordinate fractions. pc_camera and render_pitch hold this PC presentation state; render_view.camera retains the original reference camera, and player aim remains unchanged. The PC renderer and sky cache consume the presentation pitch, including convergence at level aim.

Third-person render snapshots hide type96 particles carrying flag0x2000, the five original muzzle-flash families (particle IDs8–12). The snapshot clears fixed-screen mode and sets invisibility because the reference fixed-screen projector bypasses the normal invisibility check. Actual entity flags, particles, animation lifetime and weapon state remain unchanged. Returning to first person restores normal snapshots immediately, including flashes already alive during the H toggle. World impacts and projectiles retain their normal presentation.
