Weapon 7 conversion and periodic drain

The earlier description of type 69 as freezing an enemy was incorrect. The recovered instructions implement enemy retargeting and periodic health drain. There is no demonstrated freeze/thaw transition, and no missing flag-400 freeze gate should be invented in the entity dispatcher.

The original impact routine 08010A20 accepts enemy types CD through D6 without flag 400, sets that flag at 08010C74, and writes 30 to entity +34 at 08010C78. It does not overwrite the separate halfword +36 or the target-selection timer +56. Allocation of its attached E2 effect follows these mutations; allocation exhaustion does not undo conversion.

Shared navigation 0801288C checks flag 400 at 080128D6–080128DE. If set, it subtracts ticks from +56. A null target, signed timer at or below zero, or target flag 20 causes a fresh nearest-enemy search 080036C4, with timer reset to 600. Entity +20 stores the selected target. The nearest search considers other CD–D6 bodies without flag 20, in original active-pool order; it does not exclude other converted enemies. A missing target falls back to the player. This selection happens after the existing player-sector rejection and flag-800 early exits, so it does not bypass those navigation gates.

The target affects path destinations. Phase zero still faces the player, as the original routine does. Type-CD's active controller additionally selects its alternate target for its proximity explosion. It keeps moving; it is not frozen. 08012638 still performs contact damage only when the current blocking entity has player flag 10: 20 damage for most types, 40 for D0/D6. Conversion does not introduce a universal team-based damage exclusion.

08012E1C interprets +34 as the nonzero drain period and +36 as its current countdown. It subtracts the unsigned tick byte with halfword wrapping. Only a strictly negative signed result decrements signed health by one. Surviving health reloads +36 from +34; nonpositive health sets +36=0 and body flag 1 for the controller's ordinary hit/death path. Period 30 therefore produces a 31-call cadence when ticks is one and the freshly reloaded countdown is 30. Neither expiry clears conversion flag 400 nor restores a previous target.

This address inspection does not claim that all those controllers are ported. The native shared navigation and drain routines were already recovered and differentially validated. The integration bug was field ownership: projectile impact wrote actor.parameter34, while the CD runtime used an unrelated controller.drain_period copy. SsaScriptActor now owns canonical parameter34 and parameter36; the CD runtime imports both at initialization and frame entry and exports both after the call, including error paths. Existing tests that set drain state now use those canonical actor fields.

test_projectile_conversion.py passes 686 ASan/UBSan assertions on real map 0: an actual weapon-7 shot converts a scripted CD, preserves an initial countdown of 17, and creates the original attached effect. The converted CD runs 49 updates with actual native visibility, nearest-enemy, path, world movement and combat. It moves in 48 updates, selects another CD, refreshes its target timer, and drains health twice with strict-negative timing. A real native damage call weakens it before the second drain triggers its ordinary death/explosion/group notification. The attached effect follows for 45 updates and is removed after the target gets flag 20. An injected animation-observer failure verifies that the countdown remains synchronized after a partial update. Corpse animation completion is controlled by the test; animation playback is not claimed.

The preexisting CD-runtime and native-enemy-query integration suites also pass after the canonical-field migration: 59,319 and 53,384 assertions respectively, both covering all 16 maps. The new result and source hashes are recorded in native-projectile-conversion-validation.json. These are native integration assertions, not additional ROM comparisons or exact compiler matches.

Requested PC behavior speed

The playable frontend enables fast_converted on entity and actor-animation runtimes. A living CD..D6 with flag400 gets two complete controller updates per world tick, including walking, facing, navigation, attack timers and periodic drain. Its actor animation likewise advances twice. Per-hit damage and original period/countdown fields retain their values; drain follows this doubled behavior clock too. Projectiles, player input, music and other actors keep their normal clock. Paused frames advance neither pass. A death/release/error in the first pass stops the second; dead actors and corpse animation/expiry use normal time.

Default runtime policies remain off, preserving the original recovery APIs. The original conversion integration still passes686 checks; entity scheduling passes85,982 and actor/corpse playback75,088. The extended special-weapon suite fires a real Prion shot at each of all ten eligible types, then verifies one versus two actual walks/retarget/drain/animation increments, unchanged unconverted actors, pause and one-tick corpse expiry. GCC sanitizers and MSVC agree.
