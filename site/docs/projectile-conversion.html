Weapon 7 conversion and periodic drain

Healthy enemy targeting (2026-10-21)

The existing frontend nearest_converted policy now also selects targets for healthy enemies, including both bosses. They compare living players with living Prion-affected enemies using the same 3D body-center distance; other healthy enemies are excluded. Converted actors still consider every living combatant. Selection refreshes each behavior pass, without waiting for the old retarget timer. Equal distances retain active-pool order with no explicit player bias.

The selected actor drives facing, pursuit, attack checks and projectile direction. The opt-in prion_targets controller field permits an unconverted ranged actor to fire when its aim trace reaches its selected Prion target. Ordinary allies do not satisfy that new attack gate. Both boss projectile paths use the selected actor too. If a charging CE contacts Sam while pursuing a Prion target, knockback still applies to the actual player contact. The temporary controller player projection is restored after each pass. Damage, conversion health, converted behavior speed and ranged tactics retain their existing policies below.

The targeting regression covers healthy actors choosing and switching between Sam and Prion enemies, healthy ally/dead/corpse exclusion, vertical distance, multiple converted candidates, no live targets, all six ordinary ranged types, both bosses firing away from Sam toward a converted target, and the policy-off original facing behavior. No campaign playthrough is claimed.

Validation: 8,083 targeting/combat assertions over 662 frames, including 114 healthy-enemy cases and 14 ranged/boss shots. The default-off controller suites retain 27,738 ROM comparisons; all eight focused CTests and the 415 boss self-projectile checks pass. The Windows frontend and all 128 libraries rebuild.

PC Prion balance (2026-10-21)

The playable adapter enables combat.prion_balance. A successful first Prion conversion doubles the victim's current remaining health, once, before E2 allocation. Positive health saturates at32767; dead actors are not revived, and another hit on an already converted actor does not boost it again. The original conversion flag, drain period/countdown and effect-allocation failure semantics remain intact. The recovered default-off runtime preserves ROM behavior.

The subsequent user correction removes the conversion-based damage multipliers. Melee, projectiles and explosions now retain their normal damage regardless of whether either enemy is Prion-affected. Contact deals20 normally,40 for D0/D6; there is no half-melee or double-other-damage adjustment. This also applies when both enemies are converted. Original splash attenuation, difficulty scaling, player combat and boss self-projectile immunity remain intact. The current-health boost, targeting and pursuit/ranged policies are unchanged. The damage matrix checks all four conversion combinations with the policy both enabled and disabled.

Converted enemies chasing other enemies use half their previous movement coefficient. The six ranged types CF/D1/D2/D3/D4/D6 stop advancing when within 768 units and a native aim trace reaches their selected enemy. They keep facing and firing; occluded/out-of-range targets still cause pursuit. Gravity and contact queries continue while holding position, so nearby melee remains possible. Melee-only types keep approaching at the reduced speed. The existing two behavior/animation updates per tick, firing cooldowns and drain clock are unchanged. Pursuit of the player is unchanged.

The earlier description of type 69 as freezing an enemy was incorrect. The recovered instructions implement enemy retargeting and periodic health drain. There is no demonstrated freeze/thaw transition, and no missing flag-400 freeze gate should be invented in the entity dispatcher.

The original impact routine 08010A20 accepts enemy types CD through D6 without flag 400, sets that flag at 08010C74, and writes 30 to entity +34 at 08010C78. It does not overwrite the separate halfword +36 or the target-selection timer +56. Allocation of its attached E2 effect follows these mutations; allocation exhaustion does not undo conversion.

Shared navigation 0801288C checks flag 400 at 080128D6–080128DE. If set, it subtracts ticks from +56. A null target, signed timer at or below zero, or target flag 20 causes a fresh nearest-enemy search 080036C4, with timer reset to 600. Entity +20 stores the selected target. The nearest search considers other CD–D6 bodies without flag 20, in original active-pool order; it does not exclude other converted enemies. A missing target falls back to the player. This selection happens after the existing player-sector rejection and flag-800 early exits, so it does not bypass those navigation gates.

The target affects path destinations. Phase zero still faces the player, as the original routine does. Type-CD's active controller additionally selects its alternate target for its proximity explosion. It keeps moving; it is not frozen. 08012638 still performs contact damage only when the current blocking entity has player flag 10: 20 damage for most types, 40 for D0/D6. Conversion does not introduce a universal team-based damage exclusion.

08012E1C interprets +34 as the nonzero drain period and +36 as its current countdown. It subtracts the unsigned tick byte with halfword wrapping. Only a strictly negative signed result decrements signed health by one. Surviving health reloads +36 from +34; nonpositive health sets +36=0 and body flag 1 for the controller's ordinary hit/death path. Period 30 therefore produces a 31-call cadence when ticks is one and the freshly reloaded countdown is 30. Neither expiry clears conversion flag 400 nor restores a previous target.

Requested PC behavior speed

The playable frontend enables fast_converted on entity and actor-animation runtimes. A living CD..D6 with flag400 gets two complete controller updates per world tick, including walking, facing, navigation, attack timers and periodic drain. Its actor animation likewise advances twice. Per-hit damage and original period/countdown fields retain their values; drain follows this doubled behavior clock too. Projectiles, player input, music and other actors keep their normal clock. Paused frames advance neither pass. A death/release/error in the first pass stops the second; dead actors and corpse animation/expiry use normal time.

Requested PC nearest-target behavior

The playable frontend now enables entities.nearest_converted. Before each living converted controller pass, it selects the nearest living combatant from the active pool by three-dimensional body-center distance. Candidates include players, ordinary enemies, bosses and other converted enemies. Self, dead actors, corpses, items and projectiles are excluded. Equal distances retain active-pool order, with no player preference. Selection refreshes every behavior pass, including both passes of the existing doubled behavior clock, rather than retaining the original 600-tick target preference.

The controller temporarily sees the chosen actor as its target for facing, visibility, distance checks, reactions and attacks. The real player fields are restored after the call, including errors. Converted ranged attacks launch toward the selected actor. Enemy melee contact is hostile whenever either body has conversion flag400, so converted enemies can both deal and receive melee damage, including contact with an enemy other than their selected target. The existing combat path applies20 damage, or40 from D0/D6. Each walking update captures its actual enemy collision before a crowd sidestep can replace it; successful sliding therefore no longer suppresses melee. Contact is reset for each move and runtime call, so stale blocking pointers cannot cause a hit. Self, player bodies, corpses, released slots and nonpositive canonical target health are rejected by this additional enemy-only path. Ordinary enemy pairs remain nonhostile, and the original player-contact path is unchanged. Native floor hazards run first. With no living candidates, the target clears and the actor does not attack.

This is a requested PC policy. The recovered navigation, contact and controller routines retain their original behavior when the runtime option is off. The healthy-enemy extension above supersedes the earlier player-only policy. The existing behavior-clock fixture explicitly disables nearest selection to isolate its timing assertions.
