Sprite shadows, 2026-10-21

Graphics > Sprite shadows independently enables size-scaled oval sprites for visible players, enemies/bosses/corpses and E1 pickup items. Default is Off, including Clear Data and migration from older saves. Shadows do not depend on Surface decals. Settings v19 stores the toggle in byte10 bit7, retaining the 16-byte CRC record and v1..18 migration. Existing preferences are preserved.

Graphics keeps display/renderer/aspect/mip rows at24/40/56/72; visual toggles occupy88/96/104/112, with Back at144. Mouse, keyboard and localized labels use the same positions. Extras is unchanged.

Sprite composition

The earlier floor-projected sampling pass has been removed. Shadows are now ordinary transparent upright billboards, centered at their subject's ground position and flattened into an oval. Each shadow is submitted immediately before its subject's base sprite, followed by any player-color overlay. The subject always covers its own shadow. They use painter ordering and wall silhouettes, without floor polygons, world-depth testing or a depth bias that can flicker.

The stored32x32 four-level mask in sprite_shadow_texture.inc supplies a32x8 column-major indexed image. Each subject has distinct, stable texture storage through the draw, including GPU submission and finish. Transparent zero leaves the scene untouched; three shades use the original palette at11/22/33% darkening of the receiving floor color. Palette fades remain live. Overlapping shadow sprites follow subject order rather than accumulating darkening.

Native collision radius sets the size. Zero-radius pickups derive size from the original resource's first image and original mip scale. The requested 25% enlargement uses radius7.5..120 world units before airborne adjustment and integer rounding. Rising subjects shrink/lighten their shadow, disappearing at256 units above the live sector floor. Corpses retain shadows while their sprites are visible. Hidden first-person player bodies do not submit a main-view shadow; the exposed third-person/reflected body does. Projectiles, muzzle flashes, particles, ceilings, sky and water are excluded.

GBA attaches a draw callback to the original actor queue. It draws the oval immediately before the matching base projection, without consuming any of the 32 actor slots or changing original record layouts. Its expanded horizontal bounds get independent wall clips using the original inverse-depth, segment-side, body-height and signed opening rules. Fixed-screen and secondary body layers do not duplicate shadows. Existing queue/scene APIs retain their original behavior when no callback is supplied.

Software and Vulkan use the existing transparent actor material and shared wall silhouette clipping. Reflections use the same billboard submission. Preparing up to100 sprites uses bounded draw-local storage, no heap allocations, gameplay changes or RNG. This works in GBA rendering mode; it is not a newly benchmarked ARM hardware build.

Validation

tests/test_sprite_shadows.py covers classification, enlarged sizes, airborne behavior, transparent artwork, closed/open GBA wall clips, actual GBA On/Off composition, repeated-draw stability and native On/Off restoration, under ASan/UBSan with leak checking. Current counts/hashes are recorded in sprite-shadows-validation.json.

The shared sprite_shadow_fixture.h runs against both Software and Vulkan: a low subject sprite overlaps its broad shadow at three pitches/FOV choices, and every subject pixel must survive unchanged. Disabling shadows must restore the baseline exactly. Vulkan validation passes30 paired scene draws with zero errors. The native capture was inspected: the pickup has a flat oval below it, with its own pixels in front. The original actor-queue regression and focused native tests also run. No campaign playthrough is part of this change.
