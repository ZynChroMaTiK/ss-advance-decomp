Optional floor and ceiling decals

Extras > Surface decals is saved and defaults Off. It adds new native artwork, not recovered GBA content. Floors now have very subtle connected dirt/stain tiles; ceilings use a different, lighter water-stain pattern. The old cracks, gouges and chipped plaster are removed. One toggle controls both. Sky remains undecorated.

surface_decals.c builds four fixed texture banks of49 tiles per surface, each 128x128. They sample at two world units per texel, matching the base wall textures' sampling density. Each tile now spans256 world units, twice its earlier width and height, without changing texel density. The set contains an empty tile plus47 occupied pieces: single stains, caps, straights, inner/outer corners, edges, junctions and a full interior tile. Diagonal neighbors connect internal corners without introducing tile seams; both floor and ceiling layouts include full connecting pieces. Diagonal support blends into the curved shoulders with a smooth two-axis weight. The former hard whole-quadrant fill caused rectangular density steps inside the tile artwork; it is removed. The smooth corner artwork is independent of placement probabilities and bank selection. The tile library is fixed across levels. Each map cell selects one of four texture banks, varying contours, interior gaps and speckles. All banks share border pixels; differences fade in over16 texels inside a piece, preserving exact joins for every neighboring bank combination. Fixed cluster stamps, with seeded random selection and orientation, place stains on a64x64 tile grid. Cluster activation is now100% for floors and95% for ceilings, increased from90%/70%, so more stain pieces replace empty cells. These are cluster probabilities, not the fraction of all tiles covered by stains. Matching corner/edge/full pieces are chosen from occupied neighbors. There are no ellipse fields, coordinate warping, bilinear masks or per-pixel generated shapes. Sampling reads the selected tile directly at its original texel coordinates. The grid repeats after16384 world units; ceiling placement is less dense.

All tile pixels are binary: unchanged surface or one darker original-palette shade, targeting6% attenuation. This gives at most two colors per underlying surface, including feathered edges, noise and distance fading. Soft outlines are baked into the tiles with unordered full-tile random thresholds. The former 16x16 ranked dither lattice is removed: random speckles allow natural neighboring clusters instead of favoring alternating pixels. The feather spans the full edge profile instead of saturating into a fill at low coverage. Wider profiles soften exposed boundaries. Periodic negative noise cuts irregular clean patches through the interiors, and interior dithering leaves the underlying surface visible even in full connecting pieces. There are no solid stain fills. The latest density adjustment doubles the scale of the erosion detail and lowers its strength: openings are fewer and larger, with denser remaining stain marks. The curved shoulders are inset farther from exposed corners for rounder outlines. No intermediate shade levels or interpolated colors are added. Palette matching retains fractional targets; the actual darker color depends on the chapter palette. Matching now requires a genuinely darker, distinct opaque palette color rather than accepting an unchanged surface color. This makes the artwork visible on every solid campaign floor/ceiling material, including dark ceilings. A pure black plane uses the nearest distinct opaque shade if no darker color exists. Blue-dominant floor colors used for water exclude stains and noise; ceiling colors remain eligible. Water classification uses the original unfaded palette so damage flashes and fades cannot change eligibility. Sky remains excluded.

An additional faint noise tile is selected for approximately one quarter of otherwise empty grid cells. Its sparse speckles cover about1.5% of the floor tile and1% of the ceiling tile, using the same single decal shade. Its borders are clean and it never replaces a stain tile. Other empty cells remain undecorated. Each bank contains49 tiles (48 stain/empty pieces plus noise). All artwork and thresholds are stationary and independent of level/game RNG. Original palette effects still apply; shading never substitutes transparent/sky index0. Coordinates include negative world positions and stay fixed as the camera moves. Stain intensity fades after768 units and disappears beyond1536 units to limit minification noise. No actors, collision bodies, gameplay timers or RNG state are allocated or advanced by rendering.

The software renderer samples within its existing floor/ceiling passes. Looking up/down uses world-space UVs interpolated with reciprocal depth. Depth and occlusion stay unchanged; this is not a screen-space overlay. The reference GBA renderer reuses its original visible plane intervals before actors, reconstructing floor/ceiling world coordinates at the120x120 scene resolution. Original plane rendering functions are untouched. With the option Off, original rendering is retained in both paths.

Water uses randomly placed, animated glints instead of stains when Surface decals is enabled. Four fixed8x8 star sprites pulse over24 game ticks, with independent position, phase and eligibility per64-world-unit patch and128-tick epoch. The game clock freezes them on pause; rendering does not consume gameplay RNG. Glints fade after768 units and disappear at1536.

Water also has non-glint animated decals: expanding broken ripple rings and short wavelets, using two fixed banks of six16x16 frames in water_ripples.inc. They sample at two world units per texel. Independently seeded64-unit patches choose their position, variant, orientation and phase; two thirds are eligible per256-tick epoch. Each decal animates for72 game ticks,12 ticks per frame, then disappears before its next randomized appearance. A darker water-palette shade targets20% attenuation, distinct from the bright glints. Surface decals controls both water animation types, with no new setting or save-format change. Ripples use the same pause-safe game clock and768..1536 distance fade, without advancing game RNG or entities.

Extras > Water reflections is a separate saved toggle, default Off. Both the software and GBA display modes now use a second render of the3D scene. The old reflection ray tracer and its world-grid cache are removed. The camera eye is mirrored across each visible water height, pitch is reversed, and the resulting scene image is flipped vertically into the visible-water stencil. Geometry crossing the water plane is clipped in3D before near clipping and projection; the water plane itself is excluded. The shared polygon scene pass renders current walls, ceilings, sky and world actor sprites, including their current animation and vertical offsets. HUD, first-person weapon/flash sprites and recursive reflections are excluded. This renders an independent scene from the mirrored eye; it does not flip or reuse the primary framebuffer. One scene pass runs per distinct visible water height, with no pass when no water is visible. The reflection uses50% alpha blending with the original water RGB555 color: each channel is (base*(510-fade)+reflected*fade)/510, rounded to the nearest integer. At full strength this is an equal mix of the water and reflected scene; the distance fade reduces reflection opacity toward zero. Both dark and bright scenery contribute, replacing the previous screen/additive blend. GBA output selects the nearest opaque original-palette colors. GBA reflections use a stable4x4 two-color dither when a subtle highlight falls between palette entries; exact palette colors remain unchanged. Candidate pairs and weights are cached per RGB555 result for the frame, alongside the ordinary nearest-color cache. Glints and ripple decals bypass blending and retain their palette colors. Reflections fade after768 units and disappear at1536. Static stain artwork retains its existing two-color limit; the separate water shading layer does not alter it. The native renderer uses full viewport resolution for reflections, retaining the viewport aspect ratio and live FOV without downsampling. GBA reflections remain240x160. GBA output reuses this native polygon renderer with its original3:2 projection, sampling into the original120x120 visible plane spans with nearest original-palette colors. Its ordinary original world and actor passes remain unchanged. Both paths use center-aligned bilinear reconstruction; the visible-row optimization includes the extra sample row and clamps at all image edges. FOV, pitch, height clipping and full-resolution water stencils remain intact. The reflection backend is shared through the native-core/PC-renderer static-library component rather than duplicating geometry rendering. Each owner frees its water stencil and private reflection renderer before map teardown. Buffers and geometry caches persist between frames, and the second pass does not change gameplay entities or the main depth buffer.

Settings version12 retains the16-byte CRC format and all previous flags. Byte8 bit4 stores Water reflections, bit5 Surface decals, bit6 bobbing, bit7 aspect and bits0..3 version. Versions1..11 migrate with reflections Off; versions1..10 also default decals Off. Extras has nine logical rows, eight visible at Y16..128 with a16px stride, title Y0 and Back144. Selection scrolls by one row to reveal Water reflections; arrow clicks and mouse targets follow the scroll. Each localized row overlays its own font batch so the original128-glyph limit cannot clip the final value or Back label in longer languages.
