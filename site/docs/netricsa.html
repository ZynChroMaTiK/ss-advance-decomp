Native NETRICSA

NETRICSA now appears before the map on every level start, advance and restart. Chapter introductions lead to NETRICSA, then the map; the ending still returns to Main. Pre-level NETRICSA keeps the original animated background and music. Closing it proceeds to the map, with gameplay still at frame0.

Opening N/Tab during play instead overlays NETRICSA on a frozen copy of the current game image. Its frame, artwork and text blend over that image, with transparent space showing the game. The animated background is omitted. The RGB888 compositor preserves the current PC renderer resolution, including letterbox areas, and also supports the240x160 reference renderer. Pointer mapping accounts for both the game viewport and the NETRICSA canvas. Closing returns to gameplay, restores capture/music, and frees the captured buffers. Options > Extra > In-game NETRICSA can disable this gameplay shortcut. It defaults to On and persists; pre-level briefings remain enabled either way.

In-game presentation fades take15 ticks/250ms at60Hz through ssa_netricsa_pc_step. The original controller entry point retains its recovered timing for oracle comparisons. Closing during fade-in reverses from the current opacity. In-game opening blends from the full captured frame to a dark green world with the weapon still visible: health/ammo/boss bars, crosshair and notice sprites fade away. Closing restores them. The tint follows the same continuous opacity as the NETRICSA artwork/text, including the viewport margins. Pre-level NETRICSA retains its animated background and original fade speed through ssa_netricsa_step.

The frontend captures a second frame with weapon-only OAM built from private sprite copies; live OAM is restored after rendering. No sprite links, animation timers or cache state are changed for this capture. Both RGB888 frames remain owned by the frontend until closing; the compositor supports output aliasing either frame and uses no animated menu background.

Weapon/enemy item numbers sit only above the upper text panel, starting at two pixels right and one pixel down from its top-left grid boundary, at x90/y17, one number per8-pixel grid cell toward the right. The row stays within the panel's144-pixel width. Both lists share that alignment; hit regions follow the cells. Numbers use the original green font palette normally, white on hover and yellow for the active record, which takes precedence over hover, without any background highlight. Unavailable records are hidden and non-clickable; visible record numbers keep their stable positions. The numbers retain their original font, fades and overlay composition. The preview build/native-tests/netricsa.bmp.selectors.bmp shows all three available states. The Enemies category is dim gray and cannot be hovered or selected when no enemy records are available. The availability gate includes all twelve enemy types, including a dossier containing only the final boss. A sparse/disabled preview is build/native-tests/netricsa.bmp.disabled.bmp.

The playable frontend now opens NETRICSA with N or Tab during gameplay, including terminal gameplay states. Tab toggles it off during play; Esc/N also returns to the same world. Enter/Space cycles category/upper/lower focus. Tab also closes pre-level NETRICSA and proceeds to map view, retaining its original fade speed. Arrows or WASD navigate; Left/Right cycles acquired weapons or encountered enemies. Mouse hover selects categories; clicking focuses a panel. Wheel scrolls that panel. Clicking either half of an illustration, or wheeling over it, cycles records. Hover sounds play when selection changes. Clicking the localized "Press A to skip" prompt also closes NETRICSA, both before a level and during gameplay. Its hit region follows the original font width at x134/y9 and remains active during the prompt's blink-off phase. The click queues the normal close input, preserving each screen's fade speed, close sound, music transition and destination.

Weapons/enemies now show numbered selectors above the upper text panel, using the original small font: weapons1..10, enemies1..12. Click an available number to open its record and reset both panels' scroll/held-scroll state. The current record is highlighted; hover highlights another available number. Unavailable records are dimmed and ignore clicks, preserving acquired/encountered filtering. Keyboard and illustration/wheel cycling remain available. Selectors share the same fading/compositor and letterbox pointer transform in both screen modes.

The screen uses the original23 illustrations at0844CF48, NETRICSA tiles/map 080AAF38/080B4C20, scrolling menu background080A8368, small font080B7A60 and original metrics/charmap. It renders native240x160 RGB555 pixels, scaled by the existing letterbox/presentation service. No ROM instructions execute at runtime.

Recovered behavior

Original19C00 builds the records and submits labels;1A2C4 handles category, focus, scrolling, close and refresh. The outer1A780 screen initializes category4 and focus1, loads artwork, requests music7 (song70), updates the background, and fades. Native implementations are src/netricsa.c and include/ssa/netricsa.h.

Categories are statistics, enemies, weapons, strategic data and tactical data. The upper/lower panels use18/15 BG tiles and7/9 row scroll bounds. Text keeps ROM Latin1 accents, palette directives,2px inset and original31-byte streamed line clipping. Records reference84..93 weapon lore,116..127 enemy lore, 214+map strategic text,202+map tactical text, and a random227..236 tip. Tactical lower text uses the original136 record with its additional newline. Formatted stats include chainsaw damage, infinite pistol ammunition, the U.M.T. format without accuracy, ordinary weapon accuracy, enemy encounters/hits/kills, game totals and player status. Zero shots yields0accuracy.

Original category ordering, clamped category movement, record wrap/skips and independent held one-pixel scrolling remain. Changing category can call record dispatch twice in the same original frame; simultaneous category/record keys retain that behavior. The original first11-enemy sum gates the enemy category. An empty weapon list rejects initialization instead of hanging in the original unavailable-record scan. Blank rows beyond record end are cleared natively.

German enemy-stat record129 contains four numeric directives, although the original caller supplies three arguments. The native version safely repeats the attack count in its duplicated attacks row and puts kills in the final row. That undefined original variadic read is excluded from exact text comparison.

Frontend ownership

NETRICSA owns a value snapshot, without world pointers. Native statistics come from ssa_native_game_statistics. Prior completed-map counters accumulate in the frontend only when advancing; R retains those prior-map counters and restarts the current map. Fresh New Game/password/level-selection starts clear session totals. Current health, armor, score, owned weapons and ammo remain authoritative. Lives uses the native lifecycle's continue count. Session totals are not a new save format, score table, completion tally or continue workflow.

The game does not tick while NETRICSA owns presentation. Relative movement and held buttons are cleared on entry; focus loss clears NETRICSA held scroll. Closing restores the previous capture preference and current game music mode. The existing audio reset service flushes effect loops on entry/exit; live door and enemy loops resynchronize on subsequent world updates. Both renderers use the same NETRICSA canvas. The borrowed ROM and screen address must stay stable; embedded formatted-text pointers are repaired after initialization's copy.

The tip seed currently comes from a native frame-based LCG projection. The original global random-state coupling and full outer hardware/OAM screen chain are not established as byte/pixel-identical. This is working native presentation, not a claim that the complete game port has original feature parity.
