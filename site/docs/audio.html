Native audio integration

The fixed mix adjustments below describe Original music mode. SoundFont mode now retains every ROM sampled instrument and replaces only PSG music, with unity gain before the Music volume slider, sequence fades and overload-only peak protection. It does not inherit Original's fixed PCM boost/music cut. See audio settings for the hybrid route and focused checks.

play-native.cmd now plays original ROM sound effects and the level soundtrack. Overall music amplitude is now50% (-6.02dB) of the previous mix. This applies equally to every music sequence and PCM/PSG instrument, including menus, map, gameplay, boss and end cues. Menu clicks and player/enemy/world effects retain their existing levels. The recovered note volumes, sample-to-wave balance, timing, fades and channel ownership are unchanged; the reduction is applied at the music output before effect headroom mixing. M toggles mute. --mute starts silently; --no-music keeps effects while disabling the background song. Start/restart shows the original pre-level map with its start cue (command3). Fire confirms the map; its screen fade completes before gameplay begins with the level soundtrack (command2). Restart clears active sounds while preserving these settings. A missing or failed output device is logged and the game continues silently. The death/restart notice and level-clear screen now select their original music cues once. End-screen transitions clear old effects so frozen controllers cannot leave a weapon/enemy loop playing. The death voice is emitted afterward and can still play. --no-music suppresses the music cues and keeps effects. Original language/title/new-game menus now precede the first map. Audio starts silent on language selection; confirming it requests the original menu command8. Navigation/confirm/back/boundary effects and the menu Music On/Off setting are bound. New Game requests its original fade before transitioning to the map cue.

Footsteps now play while walking or strafing on the ground in either camera mode. The original alternating sounds13/14 follow the camera bob cadence. The native composition suppresses them without movement input, after less than 1/16 unit of horizontal displacement, or while airborne/dead. The recovered camera/physics routines remain unchanged. This avoids walking sounds while jumping, standing still or pushing against a wall.

The native jump extension now plays a non-vocal bootstep (sound14) once on actual takeoff, replacing the hurt grunt at the user's request. Landing plays a local boot impact (sound13), including stationary landings and falls. Both use the ordinary player-effect path/source1, without distance attenuation or camera-dependent panning. Held Space, rejected airborne presses, disabled jumps and death/respawn do not generate takeoff cues. The normal bob footstep is suppressed on the landing frame to avoid doubling it.

The composition snapshots player position/velocity, jump latch, health and control flags before the entity phase, then checks the result immediately before movers. A jump requires a newly consumed edge and a grounded body actually rising above its floor; a landing requires a descending airborne body to reach the floor. Movers and later scripts cannot masquerade as that contact. Original physics, camera and controller routines are unchanged. This audio is a PC extension alongside the requested jump, not an original GBA jump feature. The updated audio suite passes660209 checks under GCC ASan/UBSan/leak detection and MSVC, including eight takeoffs/eight landings across moving and stationary jumps, both camera views, legacy and source-aware sinks, held/rejected input and disabled controls. All131 populated songs still render without decoder errors or catalogue clipping. Windows audio buffering/restart checks pass with music enabled and disabled (output muted, no physical listening claim). All121 native Windows libraries/frontend rebuild; executable SHA256: 19d3155b12804e7faa20bca6d2bbaf62085c63a7bd1a65a42584443c870c422f.

Enemy, activation and impact sounds now pan and attenuate with distance from Sam. Turning or moving updates their direction. H keeps the listener at Sam's eye, rather than moving it behind him with the chase camera. Sam's weapons, footsteps and music stay centered. Pickups and doors now use world positions. Each door owns its movement loop; its stop cue cannot leave that loop running or stop another moving door. Multiple CD and CE enemies emit independent loops.

Recovered data and native playback

The ROM's08C3EAC routine indexes the136-entry song table at083E99D0. Five entries are empty;105 effects/jingles and26 music sequences are populated. Headers point to instrument banks, tracks and PCM/wave data. Music selection at08008FBC reads the14-entry campaign table at0844A9DC: level0 selects song0x73. The two remaining diagnostic maps have no entry in this table and request no music.

src/audio.c reads this data without executing ARM instructions. It handles wait and note durations, running status, ties/EOT, pattern calls/returns, repeats, jumps, tempo, instruments, key/rhythm splits, volume, pan, pitch controls and LFO parameters. Rhythm-note identity is retained separately from remapped instrument pitch so tied percussion can be released correctly. Per the latest PC audio request, player0 is reserved for music (song IDs6E..85). Each music sequence track owns one voice, replacing only its own preceding note/tail at note-on. Effects and menu cues use32 independent sequence instances and64 separate oscillators. Repeated copies of the same sound can coexist, with separate positions, clocks, envelopes and PCM/pulse/wave/noise phases. The original ROM route is only a free-slot hint; it does not reserve exclusive hardware voices. Footsteps13/14 therefore cannot replace the soundtrack, and still work with --no-music. Output is signed stereo PCM with linear sample resampling and pulse,32-step wave and LFSR noise synthesis.

Effect instances remain occupied through release/echo, even after the sequence ends. Local centered effects have priority over positioned enemy/world effects at both instance and oscillator capacity, including their release/echo tails. Allocation first uses free slots, then recycles world effects before local ones. Within a class, old instances and released/old notes yield first. Incoming world sounds are dropped if all eligible storage belongs to local sounds. Music is never a candidate. The native APIs classify NULL-position play as local; world play_at/source positions remain lower priority even after their emitter retires. Stop(song) stops every instance of that ID, without affecting other IDs/music. Move(song,point) updates the newest positional instance without restarting it. These legacy ID-only calls remain available for old sinks and diagnostics. The Windows composition now uses the source API: each numeric emitter identity owns its effects independently. PLAY permits overlapping one-shots; LOOP ensures one instance per(source,song), moving it without a restart; MOVE updates every positional sound attached to that source; STOP affects only its matching sound (or all of that source with the song-count sentinel). RETIRE ends its loops and detaches one-shots at their final position, retaining death tails. No live gameplay pointer crosses the audio worker boundary.

PSG voices now follow instrument-defined attack, decay, sustain and release instead of holding full volume and applying a generic fade. Rates count64Hz steps; sustain uses4-bit levels, and zero rates skip phases. A fractional sample clock keeps this timing independent of render-buffer boundaries. Envelope peaks now follow the driver's integer volume goal instead of always reaching15. The public MP2K CgbSound implementation was used as an additional reference for the instrument envelope format.

The CD08/CD09 extension commands now set pseudo-echo volume and length. Each new voice copies these settings, so a later track change cannot change an already playing note's tail. This holds the oscillator at a low level after release; it does not replay a delayed recording. PCM envelopes now use the driver's8-bit attack/decay/sustain/release arithmetic, including the exact release threshold and sustain-zero transition into the tail. Tail length counts sound frames independently of tempo and PSG's64Hz envelope clock. PSG tails use ceil(volume_goal*echo_volume/256), including quieter notes. Stop/restart clears tails immediately; mute advances them silently.

Wave bass pitch and quieter wave levels

The first-level bass uses music-bank wave instrument1 (also shared by7), with its32-step waveform at083E9980. The former host renderer incorrectly used the pulse oscillator's frequency for wave instruments. Original C4880 produces the same period register for both kinds, but the wave's32-step hardware cycle runs at half the pulse frequency. Instruments with bit8 set also round that period according to SOUNDBIAS40 (C4D18..C4D4C). Native pulse and wave pitch now use the actual ROM period tables at083E8E90/083E8F14, including low-note clamping, fractional pitch and register rounding.

The earlier wave gain conversion selected the correct DAC setting but then scaled a centered sample smoothly. The DAC instead truncates the raw nibble: 25% maps15 to3,50% to7,75% to11, and100% to15. This made25% output25% too loud and50% output about7% too loud. The corrected path applies that integer conversion before centering, with no instrument-specific normalization. Hardware period and nibble semantics are cross-checked against the original mGBA PSG implementation.

At first-level volume105, bass note40 now uses41.21761Hz instead of about82Hz. Velocity44 selects goal4/25% DAC and1536 peak-to-peak host units (formerly1920); velocity108 selects goal10/75% DAC and5632 units (formerly5760). This correction applies to every wave instrument, including the other waveform shapes.

tests/test_audio_wave.py checks all six music-bank wave instruments (1,5,7,18,24,32), three distinct waveforms,960 original CgbSound note-on cases, 3,840 original pitch-register cases across both pulse channels and wave, and every combination of16 envelope levels and16 sample nibbles. Note-on cases cover full/quiet velocities, four track volumes and low/high keys. The original ROM supplies wave DAC registers, envelope goals and source samples; hardware nibble arithmetic is checked independently. audio-wave-validation.json records the bass measurements and hashes. The shared GCC/MSVC C fixture also checks25/50/75/100% wave output and the corrected wave half-cycle boundary.

tests/test_audio_channels.py compares the archived original allocator against 12,000 ROM channel-ownership cases, explicitly marked as a reference unused by the PC mixer. Its6,000 volume cases still exercise the production per-note arithmetic. It also executes the original SoundMode channel/master setup. ARM execution is test-only. audio-channels-validation.json records that scope. tests/audio_concurrency.inc exercises actual PC note setup and rendering for all five oscillator types: independent tracks, duplicate positioned effects, music-only plus effects-only stem summation, music state/phase preservation, same-track music note replacement, effect-note polyphony, pool saturation, stop isolation and release-tail ownership. Catalogue checks reject overlapping voices on any music track. Footsteps are also compared against music-only oscillator state across three seconds of real level music.

Original PCM envelope logic at080C32EE..080C3354 and extension dispatch at 080C51F8,080C52D8,080C52E4 were checked directly in the supplied ROM. tests/test_audio_echo.py compares18,307 PCM envelope steps/lifetime cases and 512 extension-command cases against those instructions, using ARM only as a test oracle. The MP2K mixer and CgbSound reference above also document the format. This does not implement the separate global GBA reverb mixer.

The sample header and command layouts were checked against the supplied driver; the public MP2K structure definitions and command definitions were additional format references. This implementation is new host code, not a byte-identical recovery of the original hardware mixer. No third-party runtime library, emulator, downloaded soundtrack or extracted audio package is required.

SsaNativeGame.audio_ops is an optional presentation sink. Existing weapon, enemy, pickup, mover, particle and player callbacks now dispatch play/stop events to it. Device success and song preemption do not change gameplay return values. An unbound sink retains the prior headless behavior for original subsystem tests. The remaining original menu dispatcher remains future work; recovered pages and unsupported destinations are described in menu.md.

Music queue, fades and intro return

The music backend now reconstructs the queue/fade policy at08008C04,08008D30 and08008E74. New requests replace the pending song and commit at a sound-update frame. An explicitly requested fade holds them until its volume reaches zero. Requests do not start a fade on their own. Initial playback on an empty player starts immediately in the PC backend. The public ssa_audio_fade entry point starts the recovered global fade; repeated calls leave an existing fade running. The fade applies to music and effects, including the extra PC footstep player.

The original base volume is65500 (FFDC). Each decrement removes4093 (base>>4), so17 decrements reach zero:16 leave a residual12. The interval byte is capped at15 and stored in both nibbles of the countdown. A pending song starts on the following sound frame and restores the base volume. Gain follows the driver's rounded volume control, without resetting the sequencer while fading. Stop can cancel a matching pending request; restart clears the queue/fade and restores gain. Muting advances both the fade and queued changes silently. The remaining game-transition call sites for requesting fades are not all connected yet; the device check explicitly exercises the backend fade path.

Original09120 returns modes1 and20 to mode2 when the music player finishes, only when music is enabled. This does not describe the pre-level map screen: original01ACBC explicitly requests command3, which selects the same84/85 songs but does not automatically return. The map waits for fire and original fades; gameplay initialization at020A0 requests command2. The Windows frontend now follows that separation. It flushes queued map buffers and resets fade/voices at the handoff, so the map cue cannot leak into gameplay. See map-view.md. Modes9/10 remain terminal and do not restart the level song after their cue finishes. Diagnostic maps14/15 have no campaign music entry and cannot index beyond its table.

tests/test_audio_policy.py compares8,360 frame steps,166 requests,1,552 fade starts and2,688 automatic-return cases with isolated original routines. It includes complete fades at every interval, all interval-byte values, pause-bit conditions and random states. This is12,766 ROM comparisons, with ARM execution confined to the test oracle. Sampled-tone fixtures separately check actual gain, silence at fade completion, pending replacements, buffer-size invariance, stop/reset and mute. Four real ROM cues cover both intro families and both post-boss choices through their return to campaign music.

Reverb reconnaissance found the init mode0092D300 at080C3E48, whose low byte does not enable reverb, after sound-info zeroing. The only other direct mode-set call is song start080C46AE, gated by a header flag; every supplied header has reverb0. The port preserves the resulting dry output. It has not added a global reverb effect. analysis/inspect_audio_policy.py records the direct call-site scan and init/header settings. The MP2K mode setter provides additional format context.

PC spatial playback

The sound-start boundary at08008D5C takes a sound ID, without source coordinates. The new spatial model is a PC feature, not a matching recovery of that routine. Enemy/CD controllers and activation callbacks supply their entity positions; impact/explosion particles supply the particle's position. Pickups use the same unpositioned local playback path as player sounds. Enemy sounds attach to a numeric lifetime identity and follow the body's center. Each live enemy is updated after the simulation tick. Retiring an actor stops its loops and freezes its remaining one-shots; slot reuse obtains a new identity. Particle/impact one-shots copy the emission point.

src/native_audio.inc observes the existing mover records without altering the ROM-verified mover algorithms. The centroid of each sector's boundary endpoints supplies XY, and the current floor/ceiling midpoint supplies Z. Flag8 identifies active movement. Commands ensure a single0x67 loop for that mover;0x68 stops only its loop and plays the finish cue at that mover's position. Post-tick reconciliation also retires loops when movement stops or a terminal screen freezes the world. Repeated open/close/reverse commands cannot accumulate loops. Other doors and enemies using the same sound retain their own instances.

Player line interactions have a second sound path: player_line -> player_sound_id -> SsaLevelPlayerOps.sound. The native player_sound callback must forward IDs67/68 to mover_sound. Treating these as local player effects created a second tied67 sequence on source1 for every opened door. Mover stop callbacks could stop only their own copy, leaving all player-owned copies playing until the level-end clear. The previous paired-mover test exercised the mover callback alone and missed this path.

The player-door regression now activates a real map line through that adapter, runs three opening/closing cycles, renders the finite finish cue to completion and requires exact zero PCM afterward, including repeated use of an open door. It failed on the prior code. The full normal-input route additionally rejects every67 instance without a valid mover source and every67 instance whose mover has stopped: the old code accumulated14,866 frames of stray copies; the fixed route has zero stray/stopped loops across394 active mover frames. This check runs before the level-end sound clear, so terminal cleanup cannot hide the leak.

The original CD/CE controllers' canonical loop owners remain untouched for gameplay/differential parity. With the source-capable sink, its single-owner presentation callbacks are superseded by per-emitter checks of the original phase, distance and dying-state conditions. Every qualifying CD plays0x3A and every qualifying CE plays0x4E, moving and stopping independently. Original hysteresis is preserved: CD starts at1023 and continues through1024; CE starts at399 and continues through400. Local weapon stop events target the player source, so they cannot stop an enemy's copy of the same sound. Legacy sinks retain the old canonical CD/CE callback paths. Unbind/teardown retires owned loops. Other continuous enemy sequences3D/51/5F also receive loop ownership if emitted; retirement stops them instead of preserving them as finite one-shot tails.

SsaAudioListener copies Sam's eye position and yaw's right vector; it uses the original player camera, independent of chase framing and vertical aim. Stereo balance combines with each sequence track's own pan. At the listener's right, the left channel retains25% of the right channel's level; front/back/vertical sources remain balanced. Every positioned sound now uses gain 256/(256+distance), with three-dimensional distance and no hard cutoff. The old full-volume radius of256 units is removed: gain is1 at the source,1/2 at256 units and1/5 at1024. Door movement and finish cues and enemy effects share this curve. Pickup cues use local playback like player sounds, with no distance gain or positional pan; original sequence track pan is retained. Integer square roots use bounded24.8 deltas for the fullint32 coordinate range. Moving sources/listeners smooth channel gains with a10ms time constant, per sample, independent of buffer boundaries. Music and local sounds bypass this model. There is no HRTF, Doppler, wall occlusion or room reverberation.

The world-distance fixture drives the actual native mover67/68 presentation callbacks. Rendered peaks decrease at0/32/64/128/256/512/1024/ 2048/4096 units, including within the old plateau. It checks half/fifth output, vertical distance and stereo direction. Pickup35 callbacks instead render identical PCM to local playback across item/listener positions and listener yaw, with both source-aware and legacy sinks. Existing unpositioned reference fixtures retain the same output gains. The normal-input completion still rejects both stray and stopped-mover door loops.

Independent emitters and concurrent effects intentionally supersede the original shared-player preemption and08D5C per-frame SFX gate.

Windows output and ownership

platform/win32/audio_win32.c sends48kHz stereo16-bit output to the default Windows device using three800-frame buffers, a nominal50ms queue. A worker waits on the device's manual-reset CALLBACK_EVENT and a separate stop event, refilling completed buffers immediately and independently of UI activity. Reset the completion event before scanning all headers so completions during the scan cannot be lost. Preserve its handle through waveOutReset/waveOutClose; stop and join the worker before teardown. This follows the documented WinMM event callback protocol. A critical section serializes mixing, game events, mute and restart. Reset returns pending device buffers before reuse; shutdown signals and joins the worker before unpreparing/freeing storage. The ROM stays alive throughout. The Windows buffer lifetime rules are followed. Audio does not change system volume.

Normal play enables audio automatically. Existing hidden rendering/startup tests stay silent unless --audio-test explicitly enables the device path.
