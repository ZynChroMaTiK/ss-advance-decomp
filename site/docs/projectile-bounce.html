Projectile wall reflection

projectile_bounce.c recovers full 08004718 and its 0800468C wall predicate using the native wall-ray traversal. Each update traces three leading/trailing corner rays, chosen from the signs of X/Y velocity. All three traces run even after a blocking line is found. The nearest strictly smaller fraction wins; previous fraction/line and ties retain original behavior. No position is moved.

One-sided lines pass when the body lies on their reverse side. Two-sided lines pass only when opening top is at least wrapped Z+height AND opening bottom is strictly less than Z. Equality at the floor blocks. Opening state survives between rays; one-sided predicates do not rewrite it. Fraction starts at 0x10001, and values at most 0x10000 produce reflection.

Reflection quantizes displacement to signed whole units before computing its angle/length. The outgoing angle is twice the line tangent minus the movement angle. Native sine/cosine and fixed division reproduce original velocity, including zero ticks and wrapped height tests. This is original behavior, not a floating-point physics replacement.

Initialize one dedicated SsaProjectileBounce against a loaded map. Keep the map stable, free the scratch before unloading, and do not share enemy sight, movement or camera scratch. Nested bounce, ray, wall-collector or traversal operations are rejected. Init/free return 1/-1; update returns 1 for reflection, 0 for no blocking wall, -1 for invalid integration.

tests/test_projectile_bounce_rom.py passes 16,384 complete original routine comparisons across all 16 maps: 5,141 reflections and 11,243 clear updates. It executes all 49,152 original wall rays and 19,377 wall predicates, comparing complete entity bytes, current/previous blocking lines and fractions, opening globals and traversal blocking fraction. Only the BIOS integer division service is substituted. Maximum observed intercept count is 8, within the original 32-record scratch. 144 native guard checks cover nested scratch and repeated initialization/free. Strict compiler warnings and undefined-behavior sanitizer pass. Evidence and source hashes are recorded in projectile-bounce-validation.json.
